# Talk track: Vercel Drop, no account needed (2 to 3 minutes)

Live demo: https://vercel-drop-walkthrough.vercel.app

Simulated demo. Quollridge Energy is a fictional company and every number is illustrative.

---

**[Step 1, "The storm". Point at the 38,400.]**

Saturday, 4:40 in the afternoon. A storm rolls through the Hunter and the Central Coast and 38,400 customers lose power. Every single one of them wants to know one thing: when is it coming back?

**[Point at the ops channel.]**

So Priya, the duty ops lead, does what a lot of teams do now. She asks an AI agent: build me a customer outage status page, something I can text to customers. Six minutes later, it's done.

Here's the thing I want you to notice. Building the page was never the hard part.

**[Click Next. Step 2, "The account wall". Let the timeline play.]**

This is how that evening goes today. Page ready at 4:52. Then the agent goes to put it online and hits a sign-up form. Sign-up sends a verification email. An agent can't click that link. Hard stop.

So it goes to a human. A ticket goes to IT. On a Saturday night, after hours. The account gets approved at 7:40, the page goes up, and the link reaches customers at 9:04pm. Four hours after the work was finished.

**[Point at the call centre counter.]**

And while that page sits in a queue, the call centre takes nearly 10,000 calls asking the one question the page already answers.

**[Click Next. Step 3, "Drop it".]**

Now the same agent, the same page, with Vercel Drop. No account. No verification email. No ticket.

**[Drag the outage-status folder onto the drop zone, or click the zone.]**

The agent drops the folder, and Vercel hands back a live URL. Thirty-eight seconds. It's 4:52 and the page is live.

**[Click Next. Step 4, "Share it".]**

Three minutes later Priya texts that link to every affected customer, pins it on the website, and puts it on the phone line. Customers get the answer on their phone instead of waiting on hold. In this example that's more than 4,300 calls the call centre never has to take.

**[Click Next. Step 5, "Claim it".]**

Now the part I love: deploy first, identity later. The page went live without an account, and it's live on a one-hour clock. Within that hour, Priya signs in.

**[Click "Sign in and claim", then Continue, then "Claim into Quollridge Ops", then Done.]**

She claims it into the company's Vercel team. Same URL, still live, and now it sits under the company's own access and governance.

**[Click "Skip ahead one hour".]**

And look at the other one. That's a draft the agent tried earlier and nobody needed. Nobody claimed it, so after an hour it's simply gone. No orphaned links lying around after the storm.

**[Click Next. Step 6, "The value".]**

So what changed? The agent was always fast. The account wall was the slow part. Take it away and the page goes from finished to live in 38 seconds instead of four hours and twelve minutes. Customers get answers more than four hours sooner, the call centre avoids about 4,300 calls, roughly $39,000 in one storm, and over six storms a year that's around $235,000.

That's what Vercel is doing here: taking the friction out of the way so the software your agents build reaches your customers right away, and lands safely in your team when someone signs in.

If your teams are already building with agents, I'd love to show you what this looks like on your own workflows.
